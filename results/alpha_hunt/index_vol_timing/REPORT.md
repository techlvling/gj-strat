# Alpha hunt: volatility-managed index premium with VRP and VIX term-structure timing (`index_vol_timing`)

All rules, variants, the survival bar and the fixes are in the docstring of `run_alpha_index_vol_timing.py`. They were fixed before the first run.
- **Strategy:** long-only index weights held close to close and rebalanced at the last close of each month (P5 and P6 also act daily).
- **Costs:** half the signal-minute spread plus slippage on every weight change, plus 3%/yr financing on the notional.
- **Periods:** design = data start (2012-03) to 2017, check = 2018-23, holdout = 2024-26 (to 2026-09), last 3 years = 2023-10..2026-09.
- **One trade = one month.** R is the net monthly return in % of equity.

**Tests: 18 (P1-P6 x Nasdaq 100, S&P 500, Nikkei 225; P0 buy-and-hold is the benchmark, not a test).**
- **Survivors of the pre-declared bar, (a) charter bar AND (b) beats buy-and-hold in check and holdout: 0.** Expected by luck: 18 x 0.006 = 0.11.
- 8 of the 18 pass (a) alone. So does plain buy-and-hold on the Nasdaq and the S&P, so (a) alone measures index beta, not timing skill.

## Verdict

**No survivor. Volatility management, VRP timing and the VIX-backwardation switch do not beat buy-and-hold out of sample on these index CFDs.**
- No candidate beats P0 in both check and holdout.
- The one that passes (b), Nikkei P4, beats P0 by +0.10%/month in check and +0.001%/month in holdout, and fails the design t (1.43).
- The only real benefit is risk shape: a smaller worst day and, on the Nasdaq, a smaller max drawdown. That is not alpha, and it is not a faster way to pass the challenge.

## Summary

- **Vol-managed exposure (P1, P2; Moreira-Muir):**
  - The full-sample Moreira-Muir alpha is positive in all three markets but never significant:
    - Nasdaq P2: +0.19%/month (t 1.20).
    - S&P P2: +0.05%/month (t 0.30).
    - Nikkei P2: +0.06%/month (t 0.52).
  - Sharpe barely moves:
    - Nasdaq: 0.93 vs 0.88.
    - S&P: 0.66 vs 0.69.
    - Nikkei: 0.62 vs 0.61.
  - Against P0, the return difference is negative in check in every market, and negative in holdout except the S&P:
    - P2 check: Nasdaq -0.30, S&P -0.19, Nikkei -0.05%/month.
    - P2 holdout: Nasdaq -0.16, S&P +0.13, Nikkei -0.24%/month.
  - This matches the out-of-sample critiques (Cederburg et al. 2020): the design-period gains (pooled P1 +0.22, P2 +0.17%/month) disappear after 2017.
  - **Where it helps (risk):**
    - Worst day: Nasdaq -7.4% vs -9.1%, S&P -7.6% vs -11.7%, Nikkei -9.8% vs -14.3%.
    - Nasdaq max drawdown: -23% vs -38%. The S&P and Nikkei drawdowns barely change.
    - 2022 was the best year for the idea: Nasdaq P2 -20.7% vs -35.1%.
  - **Where it hurts (V-shaped recoveries):**
    - 2020: Nasdaq P1 +5.7% vs +43.2%; S&P P1 -10.8% vs +12.8%.
    - Also 2019 and 2023. The weight is cut after the crash, so the portfolio misses the rebound.
  - **The monthly rebalance cannot react to a shock that starts from calm.** Several of the managed versions' worst days are calm-month blow-ups taken at high weight:
    - 2018-02-05 Volmageddon: Nasdaq P4 at w 1.92 lost -11.0%; S&P P1 at w 2.0 lost -8.4%.
    - 2016-11-09: Nikkei P1 at w 1.97 lost -9.8%.
- **VRP timing:**
  - **P3 (flat when VRP < 0, about 13% of month-ends):** lowers the mean weight and lags P0 in check in every market. Holdout: Nasdaq -0.28, S&P +0.01, Nikkei -0.39%/month.
  - **P4 (x1.5 / x0.5 by VRP tercile) is the strongest in-sample idea:** pooled design difference vs P0 +0.37%/month (t 2.12), Nasdaq design t 3.54. Then it fades:
    - Pooled: check +0.01, holdout -0.10%/month.
    - S&P: check +0.15, holdout -0.005%/month.
    - Nasdaq: check -0.21, holdout -0.28%/month.
    - It also has the deepest drawdowns: Nikkei -46%, S&P -37%.
- **P5 (daily P2 target with a 25% no-trade band):** about the same as P2. S&P check +0.19 but holdout -0.13%/month. It is the weakest variant on the Nikkei.
- **P6 (flat while VIX >= VIX3M, about 7% of days):** the worst variant.
  - Below P0 in every market's holdout: Nasdaq -0.63 (t -1.73), S&P -0.36, Nikkei -0.95%/month (t -1.93). Pooled holdout t -2.0.
  - It trades 11-21 x the equity per year. Backwardation comes after the drop, and the switch sits out the rebound days.
- **Costs:**
  - Trading costs are small: 0.06-0.33%/yr over the full sample, with the most for P6.
  - The 3%/yr financing on the notional (charter convention, the same for P0) is the main drag: 2.4-4.4%/yr over the full sample, scaling with the mean weight.
  - Levered variants pay it on more notional. This is why the S&P P1/P2 (mean w 1.3-1.5) gain little over P0.
- **Prop view (Alpha Swing):**
  - Each variant is scaled so that its worst historical day = -2.5% of equity, giving s = 0.17-0.42.
  - At that scale, no P1-P5 12-month window ever touched -10%, so the leg is safe. It is also slow: 0.17-0.44%/month over the full sample, 0.27-0.63%/month in the last 3 years.
  - +10% takes about 1.5-5 years, so it does not speed up a challenge.
  - Vol management allows a larger s for the same worst day: P2 vs P0 is Nasdaq 0.34 vs 0.28, S&P 0.33 vs 0.21, Nikkei 0.26 vs 0.18. That gives a slightly higher mean at equal worst-day risk.
  - That comparison is descriptive only. s comes from the full-sample worst day, a single observation, so it is in-sample.
- **Data and fixes (also in the docstring):**
  - Signal minutes need ticks. S&P uses live hourly bars only.
  - The series is split at gaps longer than 5 trading days: Nasdaq and Nikkei 2011-12 and Feb-May 2013, Nikkei most of 2015, S&P 2013. All variants are flat across a gap and RV21 warms up again. Partial months are dropped.
  - Months tested: Nasdaq 170 (design 65), S&P 169 (design 65), Nikkei 158 (design 53).
  - The S&P hourly data end on 2026-09-30. The final-month rule drops 2026-09, so the S&P holdout ends 2026-08.
  - The missing 2011-12 ask uses a fallback spread (Nasdaq 2.18 pt, Nikkei 5.0 pt on about 270 days).
  - VRP uses the S&P RV21 at the latest S&P close at or before the signal. The Nasdaq signal at 15:55 NY comes before the S&P's 16:00 close, so the Nasdaq uses the previous S&P close.
  - VRP is available at every included month-end.
